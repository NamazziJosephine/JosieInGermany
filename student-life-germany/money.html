Money & Work

Insurance, banking, getting around and earning next to your studies.

🩺

Health insurance

Health insurance is compulsory in Germany. Students are often able to join a public health insurer at a reduced student rate. Your university will ask for proof of insurance when you enrol.

Check with an insurer which option applies to you, especially if you come from outside the EU.

🏦

Bank account

A current account (Girokonto) is used for rent, bills and salary. Many banks offer student accounts. Some students from outside the EU also need a blocked account (Sperrkonto) as proof of funds for their visa.

Compare fees before you choose, and check what your visa requires.

🚆

Getting around

Many universities include a regional public transport ticket in the semester contribution. There is also a nationwide monthly ticket (Deutschlandticket), and some students get a discounted version.

Prices change, so always check the current price with your university or the transport operator.

💼

Working as a student

Typical options are a student job at your university, a Werkstudent role (part-time work in your field), or a mini-job. Students from outside the EU are usually limited to 140 full days or 280 half days per year.

Rules depend on your residence permit, so confirm yours with the immigration office.

Tip: keep digital copies of your passport, residence permit, insurance card and rental contract. You will be asked for them often.
